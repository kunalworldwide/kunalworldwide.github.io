Cypher 2026, the 10th edition of India's largest AI conference by Analytics India Magazine, drew 5,000+ practitioners to KTPO Whitefield. On the Expo Stage, I ran a different kind of session: no monologue, just a guessing game — "Guess the AI Bill: real incidents, real numbers."

The format was simple. I showed five real AI governance incidents from Samsung, Microsoft and more, and the audience shouted their guesses. Closest number won the round; scores settled at the CAST AI booth. Every incident carried the same lesson: the tool did not fail, nobody broke a rule — the bill, or the leak, simply arrived.

The incident files:

- **Samsung ChatGPT leaks** — engineers pasted confidential code into ChatGPT; 3 leaks in 20 days (chip source code, test code, a meeting transcript). First a 1,024-byte prompt cap, then a full ban. The data never came back.
- **The $1,200 two-hour demo** — an enterprise rolled AI coding tools out to 5,000 engineers (adoption 32% to 84% in a month), and one CTO's single two-hour demo cost $1,200. The 2026 AI budget ran out in April; the cap is now $1,500 per tool.
- **Microsoft cancelled Claude Code on June 30** — with spend at $500–2,000 per engineer per month, the CFO pulled licenses on the last day of the fiscal year so the cut lands clean in the new baseline.
- **18,033 TB of company data** flowed into AI tools in 2025 — up 93% year over year, with 410M DLP violations on ChatGPT alone, and 39.7% of everything employees paste into AI tools is sensitive.
- **The live governance gap** — 77% of knowledge workers paste data into AI tools, 82% of it via personal accounts. The gap isn't a statistic; it was sitting in the room.

The takeaway: every story ends in a ban or a cap — slower teams, same exposure, bill still real. You can't predict what AI will do, but you can own how it's used: which model the work runs on, what it costs, and what leaves the building. That's the job we built Kimchi for — open source, routes the work, caps the bill, keeps data home.
